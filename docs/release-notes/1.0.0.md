# Clackwise 1.0.0

Mechanical keyboard sounds for every key on your Mac, in every app. The clack lands with your finger.

## Highlights

- **Low latency.** About 16 ms from key event to sound on the built-in speakers and about 5.5 ms on wired
  headphones (estimated from the audio system's reported latencies). Each sound starts right at the click
  (lead-in trim), not at the quiet pre-travel noise before it.
- **Dynamics.** On MacBooks with the motion sensor, harder presses sound a little brighter and softer ones a
  little mellower; loudness moves only about ±2 dB. Subtle by design, adjustable from "Even" to "Strong".
- **Works on every Mac.** Without the sensor (M1 MacBook Air, 13" M1 MacBook Pro, Intel and desktop Macs) every
  key plays at the normal level, Dynamics is off, and Settings says so.
- **17 sound packs.** 13 with recorded key-up sounds (kbsim, Holy Panda) and 4 classic Mechvibes packs with
  synthesized key-ups. Add your own Mechvibes packs by dropping them into a folder.
- **Private.** Nothing you type is stored or sent; no network code; Input Monitoring is the only permission.
- Menu bar panel, global shortcut (⌃⌥⌘K), pause while a microphone is in use, Bluetooth/AirPlay muted by
  default (they add 100 ms or more), launch at login.

## Install

Requires macOS 14 or later (tested on macOS 27). The app is **not notarized**, so macOS blocks the first
launch: unzip, move **Clackwise.app** to Applications, open it, click **Done** (not Move to Trash), then
**System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway**. When macOS asks to receive keystrokes, click
**Open System Settings**, not the blue Deny. Step by step in the [README](https://github.com/bobk4ta/clackwise#install).

## Known limits

- The motion sensor interface is undocumented and may change with a macOS update; Clackwise then falls back to
  the normal level.
- Tested on one Mac (MacBook Air, M4). Compatibility with other MacBooks comes from public reports.
- Universal binary (Apple silicon and Intel). The Intel build was tested only under Rosetta, not on real Intel hardware.
- Signed with a self-signed identity (the Input Monitoring permission survives updates), not by Apple.

Source, build instructions and [how it works](https://github.com/bobk4ta/clackwise/blob/main/docs/HOW-IT-WORKS.md) are in the repository.
